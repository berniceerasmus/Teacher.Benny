Conditionals

Master the language of possibility, hypothetical thinking, and regret — essential for negotiation, planning, and professional decision-making.

B1 · B2 ~45 minutes Business English 8 sections

📐 Grammar at a glance

Zero Conditional

if + present simple,
present simple

General truths, rules, and automatic consequences.

If costs rise, margins fall.

1st Conditional

if + present simple,
will + infinitive

Real, likely future situations and their probable results.

If we meet the deadline, we'll get the bonus.

2nd Conditional

if + past simple,
would + infinitive

Hypothetical or unlikely present/future situations.

If we had more funding, we would expand the team.

3rd Conditional

if + past perfect,
would have + pp

Imagining a different past — things that didn't happen.

If we had acted sooner, we would have avoided the delay.

Mixed Conditional

if + past perfect,
would + infinitive

A past event with a present consequence — or vice versa.

If we had hired more staff, we wouldn't be struggling now.

Reading

Read the passage and answer the comprehension questions

B1

A regional distribution company is currently deciding whether to open a second warehouse. The operations manager presented three scenarios to the board last week.

In the first scenario, she explained that if demand continued to grow at its current rate, they would need additional storage space within eighteen months. She warned that if they delayed the decision, they would risk losing contracts to competitors who had more capacity.

In the second scenario, she argued that if the company had made the investment two years ago, they would already be operating at full capacity and turning away business. She acknowledged that the timing had not been right, but said that if they acted now, the situation was still recoverable.

The board asked what would happen if funding could not be secured. The manager replied that if that were the case, they would need to subcontract storage to a third party, which would significantly reduce their profit margins.

1. What did the manager say would happen if demand continued to grow?

2. What risk did she warn about if they delayed the decision?

3. What does the 3rd conditional in the second paragraph tell us about the investment decision?

4. Find one example of each: a 1st conditional, a 2nd conditional, and a 3rd conditional. Write them here.

✓ Suggested answers

  1. They would need additional storage space within eighteen months.
  2. They would risk losing contracts to competitors with more capacity.
  3. It tells us the investment didn't happen — it's an imaginary past that didn't occur. They would already be operating at full capacity if they had invested.
  4. 1st: "if demand continued to grow…they would need additional storage" / 2nd: "if that were the case, they would need to subcontract" / 3rd: "if they had made the investment two years ago, they would already be operating…"

Reading

A formal analytical text — read and examine conditional usage

B2

In the post-mortem report following the collapse of the joint venture, analysts identified several decision points where different choices could have altered the outcome significantly. Had due diligence been conducted more thoroughly at the outset, many of the subsequent legal complications would have been avoided.

The report argued that if the two companies had established clearer governance structures from the beginning, the disputes over intellectual property would never have escalated to the point they did. Furthermore, were a similar venture to be attempted in the future, the analysts strongly recommended that a joint steering committee be formed within the first 90 days.

One section of the report examined what would happen if the market conditions had been different. It concluded that even if demand had remained strong, the structural weaknesses in the partnership would eventually have surfaced — suggesting that the underlying problems were organisational rather than external.

The report closed by noting that unless both parties committed to a fundamentally different model of collaboration, any future attempt at partnership would be likely to produce similar results. Provided that the lessons identified were acted upon, however, the analysts believed a successful outcome remained achievable.

1. What does "Had due diligence been conducted more thoroughly" mean, and what would the result have been?

2. What does "were a similar venture to be attempted in the future" signal, and what did the analysts recommend?

3. What conclusion did the report reach about the structural weaknesses, even in a hypothetical scenario with strong demand?

4. Identify the mixed conditional in the text. Why is it mixed rather than a standard 3rd conditional?

5. Why does the writer use "unless" and "provided that" in the final paragraph rather than "if not" and "if"? What effect does the choice of these connectors create?

✓ Suggested answers

  1. It is an inverted 3rd conditional meaning "If due diligence had been conducted more thoroughly." The result: many legal complications would have been avoided.
  2. It is an inverted 2nd conditional signalling a hypothetical future scenario. The recommendation: a joint steering committee should be formed within the first 90 days.
  3. Even under strong demand, the structural weaknesses would eventually have surfaced — suggesting the problems were organisational, not market-related.
  4. "If we had launched last year, we would be in a stronger position today" — the if-clause is past (3rd conditional structure) but the result clause is present ("would be"), not "would have been." This links a past event to a present consequence, making it mixed.
  5. "Unless" is more formal and emphatic than "if not" — it highlights a non-negotiable condition. "Provided that" is also more formal than "if" and carries the sense of a prerequisite or condition that must be met. Together they give the closing paragraph a tone of formal warning and conditional optimism.
