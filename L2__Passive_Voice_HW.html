Reading

A formal business text — read and analyse passive usage

B2

It is widely acknowledged that workplace health and safety standards have improved significantly over the past two decades. Legislation has been strengthened in many countries, and it is now required by law that all potential hazards be identified and documented before work begins on any site.

At one mid-sized construction firm, a near-miss incident was reported last year by a site supervisor. It was later established that the safety briefing had not been conducted before the work was carried out — a serious procedural failure. An investigation was launched immediately, and all work on site was suspended while the findings were reviewed.

Since then, the firm has had its safety protocols completely redesigned by an external occupational health consultancy. All managers have been retrained, and new checklists must now be completed and signed off before any task is begun. It is believed that these changes will significantly reduce the risk of future incidents.

The firm has been praised by industry regulators for the speed and transparency with which the situation was handled. It is expected that the updated protocols will be adopted as a model by other companies in the sector.

1. What is now required by law in many countries, according to the text?

2. What procedural failure was identified after the incident, and what action was taken?

3. How does the text show that an external party was used to redesign the safety protocols? What structure is used?

4. Find two examples of passive reporting structures (e.g. "it is believed that...") and explain why the writer uses them instead of naming who believes or expects these things.

5. What has the firm been praised for, and by whom? Why do you think the writer uses the passive here rather than the active?
