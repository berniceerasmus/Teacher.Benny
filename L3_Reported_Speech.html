📐 Grammar at a glance

Direct → Reported

say / tell + that + backshift

When reporting what someone said, the verb tense typically shifts back one step.

"We are ready." → He said they were ready.

Tense backshift

present → past · past → past perfect

DirectReported
is / arewas / were
willwould
cancould
have donehad done
didhad done

Questions & commands

asked / wanted to know · told / asked to

Yes/No questions: asked if / whether
Wh- questions: asked what / where / when
Commands: told / asked + to + infinitive

"Can you send it?" → She asked if I could send it.

Time & place shifts

pronoun, time, place changes

today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · here → there · now → then · this → that

"I'll call you tomorrow." → He said he would call me the next day.

Reading

Read the passage and answer the comprehension questions

B1

After last week's team meeting, the operations manager sent out a summary to all staff. She explained that the company had performed well in the first quarter but said that some targets had not been met. She added that the team would need to work harder in the coming months.

During the meeting, the finance director told everyone not to overspend their budgets. He asked the department heads whether they had reviewed their forecasts, and reminded them that all expenses had to be approved in advance.

One department head asked when the new software would be available. The IT manager replied that it would be installed the following week, and promised that staff training would begin immediately after.

1. What did the operations manager say about the first quarter?

2. What did the finance director tell everyone, and what did he ask them?

3. What question did a department head ask, and what was the reply?

4. Find one example each of: a reported statement, a reported question, and a reported command. Write them here.

✓ Suggested answers

  1. She said the company had performed well but that some targets had not been met.
  2. He told everyone not to overspend. He asked whether they had reviewed their forecasts.
  3. They asked when the new software would be available. The IT manager said it would be installed the following week and that training would begin immediately after.
  4. Statement: e.g. "She explained that the company had performed well." Question: e.g. "He asked whether they had reviewed their forecasts." Command: e.g. "He told everyone not to overspend their budgets."

Reading

A more complex professional text with analytical questions

B2

Following the collapse of contract negotiations last month, a spokesperson for the company confirmed that both parties had been unable to reach an agreement on pricing terms. She acknowledged that the discussions had been difficult and admitted that certain expectations had not been communicated clearly from the outset.

The chief negotiator later insisted that the company had acted in good faith throughout, and denied having made any commitments that were not reflected in the written record. He warned that any inaccurate reporting of the situation would be challenged.

In contrast, the other party's representative claimed that the company had repeatedly changed its position during negotiations. He accused the lead team of delaying the process unnecessarily and suggested that a third-party mediator should have been brought in at an earlier stage.

A legal consultant who had observed the final round of talks urged both sides to return to the table, arguing that a mediated settlement would serve both parties better than litigation. She recommended that all future communications be conducted in writing.

1. What did the spokesperson confirm and acknowledge about the negotiations?

2. What did the chief negotiator insist, deny, and warn?

3. What did the other party's representative claim and accuse the lead team of?

4. Why does the writer use a variety of reporting verbs (confirm, acknowledge, admit, insist, deny, warn, claim, accuse, urge, recommend) rather than simply using "said" throughout? What effect does this create?

5. What did the legal consultant recommend? What structure does "recommend" take here, and why?

✓ Suggested answers

  1. She confirmed that both parties had been unable to agree on pricing. She acknowledged that discussions had been difficult and admitted expectations hadn't been communicated clearly.
  2. He insisted the company had acted in good faith; denied having made uncommitted promises; warned that inaccurate reporting would be challenged.
  3. He claimed the company had repeatedly changed its position; accused the lead team of delaying the process unnecessarily.
  4. Each reporting verb conveys the speaker's attitude, tone, and intent — "deny" implies defensiveness, "accuse" implies blame, "urge" implies urgency. Using only "said" would lose this nuance and produce flat, imprecise reporting.
  5. She recommended that all future communications be conducted in writing. "Recommend" takes a that-clause with a subjunctive (base form: "be conducted"), common in formal recommendations.

Discussion

Analytical discussion — precision in reporting verb choice

B2

Discuss these questions in depth. Focus on choosing the most precise reporting verb for each situation.

Think of a difficult workplace conversation you have witnessed or been part of. Report what was said using a range of reporting verbs — not just "said". Consider: did anyone deny, insist, warn, accuse, or admit anything?
How important is it to report speech accurately in your job? What can go wrong when messages are misreported or paraphrased imprecisely?

Have you ever had to write a summary or minutes of a meeting? How do you decide which reporting verb to use, and how much do you paraphrase versus report directly?
