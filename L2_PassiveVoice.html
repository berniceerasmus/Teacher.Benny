The Passive Voice

Learn how to shift focus away from "who did it" to "what happened" — essential for reports, processes, and formal professional communication.

B1 · B2 ~45 minutes Business English 8 sections

📐 Grammar at a glance

Active Voice

subject + verb + object

Focus is on who performs the action.

The IT team installed the new system.

Passive Voice

be + past participle (+ by + agent)

Focus shifts to the action or result; the doer becomes less important or unknown.

The new system was installed (by the IT team).

When to use it

processes · reports · formal tone

Common in business writing: procedures, policies, announcements, and when the agent is obvious, unknown, or unimportant.

The budget has been approved. Orders are processed within 48 hours.

Reading

A more complex professional text with analytical questions

B2

It is widely acknowledged that data protection has become a critical concern for organisations of all sizes. In recent years, stricter regulations have been introduced across many industries, and companies are now required to demonstrate that customer data is being handled responsibly.

At one mid-sized retail company, a full data audit was carried out last year after a minor security incident. It was discovered that several outdated systems had not been updated for over two years, and as a result, sensitive information had been left vulnerable.

Since then, a new data protection framework has been implemented. All staff are now required to complete annual training, and any data breach must be reported within 24 hours. External audits are also conducted twice a year to ensure standards are being maintained.

It is expected that similar frameworks will soon be adopted by smaller companies, although some have argued that the cost of compliance could be prohibitive for businesses with limited resources.

1. What is widely acknowledged about data protection, according to the text?

2. What was discovered during the data audit, and why had it happened?

3. Name three requirements of the new data protection framework.

4. Why does the writer use passive structures like "it is widely acknowledged" and "it is expected" rather than naming who believes these things?

5. What concern have some people raised about smaller companies adopting these frameworks?

✓ Suggested answers

  1. That it has become a critical concern for organisations of all sizes.
  2. That several outdated systems had not been updated for over two years, leaving sensitive information vulnerable; it happened after a minor security incident.
  3. Annual staff training; reporting breaches within 24 hours; external audits twice a year.
  4. These structures present the belief or expectation as general or widely held, without attributing it to a specific person or source — common in formal/academic-style business writing.
  5. That the cost of compliance could be prohibitive for businesses with limited resources.
